## Overview

Fresh spaghetti made from scratch using the KitchenAid Gourmet Pasta Press (KSMPEXTA) with its spaghetti extrusion disc. This first test used only all-purpose flour, eggs, and a little additional water. The cooked pasta was tossed with a simple garlic-butter sauce and finished with grated Parmesan and black pepper.

The pasta came out with excellent firmness and flavor. The main adjustment during preparation was adding water because the initial dough was too dry to pass through the press.

## Equipment

- KitchenAid stand mixer
- KitchenAid Gourmet Pasta Press with spaghetti disc and cutter
- Mixing bowl and flat beater
- Pot for boiling pasta
- Pan or pot for finishing the butter sauce
- Tray or shallow dish for collecting extruded pasta

## Ingredients

### Fresh Pasta

- 200 g all-purpose flour (approximately 1⅔ cups; weighing is preferable)
- 2 large eggs
- Water, added gradually as needed (exact amount **not recorded**)

### Garlic-Butter Finish

- Butter, to taste (approximately 2 tbsp is a useful starting point; exact amount **not recorded**)
- 1 garlic clove
- Finely grated Parmesan, to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- Salt, for the pasta water and final seasoning
- Reserved pasta cooking water, as needed to loosen and coat the pasta (use **only if needed**; amount not recorded)

> The pasta quantities above reflect the starting test recipe. Water was added during preparation, but the final amount was not measured. Sauce quantities other than the single garlic clove are suggested starting points rather than verified measurements from the cook.

## Instructions

1. **Mix the dough.** Add 200 g all-purpose flour to the stand mixer bowl. Beat the eggs separately, then mix them into the flour using the flat beater on low speed until the mixture forms coarse, uneven crumbs.
2. **Check hydration.** Squeeze a handful of the mixture. It should hold together when compressed without becoming sticky or forming a smooth, wet dough ball. The initial batch looked crumbly but proved too dry for the press, so additional water was incorporated gradually. Add small amounts, mixing between additions, rather than guessing a large quantity upfront.
3. **Rest briefly.** Cover the dough and let it sit for approximately 10–15 minutes to hydrate evenly. Recheck its consistency before extrusion.
4. **Set up the press.** Attach the KitchenAid Gourmet Pasta Press with the spaghetti disc and cutter. Use the operating speed specified by the attachment manual and feed small portions into the hopper; use the supplied pusher, never fingers.
5. **Extrude.** Allow the dough to pass through the press. Cut the spaghetti to the desired length and collect it in a lightly floured tray. The successful batch produced long, fairly uniform strands.
6. **Boil.** Cook the fresh spaghetti in salted boiling water. Begin checking after about 2 minutes and continue until it reaches your preferred firmness. Exact cooking time was not recorded.
7. **Prepare the sauce.** Gently cook one garlic clove in butter until fragrant without burning it. Add the cooked pasta and toss to coat, loosening with a small splash of reserved pasta water if necessary.
8. **Finish.** Add grated Parmesan and black pepper. Taste and adjust seasoning, then serve immediately.

## Kitchen Notes

### What Worked

- The press produced long, consistent spaghetti strands that held together well.
- The cooked pasta had **the ideal level of firmness** and tasted excellent.
- A single garlic clove added to the butter sauce made the finished dish notably aromatic and flavorful.
- Parmesan and black pepper provided a simple finish without overpowering the homemade pasta.
- The first use of the pasta press was a success after adjusting dough hydration.

### What Needed Adjustment

- The initial flour-and-egg mixture was **too dry to pass through the pasta press**.
- Adding water corrected the problem; the exact additional amount was not measured.
- Visual crumb texture alone was not enough to determine whether the dough would extrude successfully.

## Next Time

> These are ideas for improving repeatability, not corrections to the successful batch.

- Weigh the eggs without shells and measure every addition of water to establish a reproducible dough formula.
- Record the final hydration, rest time, press speed, and cooking time.
- Repeat the same recipe before changing flour types, then compare it against a blend containing finely milled durum semolina.
- Try another press disc, such as rigatoni or fusilli, and compare how the shape affects sauce coverage and bite.
- Measure the butter and Parmesan to make the garlic-butter finish easier to recreate.
