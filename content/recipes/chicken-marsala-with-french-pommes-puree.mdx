## Overview

Chicken Marsala pairs browned chicken with a savory mushroom sauce made from Marsala wine and pan drippings. The sauce is reduced until glossy and spoon-coating, then served with exceptionally smooth French-style mashed potatoes (*pommes purée*).

This was cooked on **November 18, 2026**, using **three chicken breasts**. The sauce and pommes purée were particularly successful. The chicken developed good color but was a little dry from searing too long.

> **Cooking record:** The finished dish and outcome are confirmed. Exact quantities for the original Marsala sauce were not preserved in the available notes. The measurements below are a practical reconstruction for three chicken breasts, **not** a claim that every amount was measured during the original cook.

## Equipment

- Large skillet (cast iron or stainless steel)
- Large saucepan for potatoes
- Potato ricer
- Tongs and a wooden spoon or spatula
- Instant-read thermometer
- Small saucepan or microwave-safe vessel to warm dairy

## Ingredients

### Chicken Marsala

- 3 boneless, skinless chicken breasts
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- 1/3 cup all-purpose flour, for a light dredge (optional if repeating the dish)
- 1–2 tbsp avocado oil or olive oil, for searing
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 8 oz (225 g) cremini or button mushrooms, sliced
- 1 small shallot, finely chopped (optional)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced (optional)
- 3/4 cup (180 ml) dry Marsala wine
- 3/4 cup (180 ml) low-sodium chicken broth
- 1–2 tsp fresh thyme leaves, or 2 sprigs (optional)
- 1–2 tbsp cold unsalted butter, to finish the sauce if needed

### French Pommes Purée

- 1 1/2 lb (680 g) Yukon Gold potatoes
- 6 tbsp (85 g) unsalted butter, cubed
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) heavy cream, warmed, plus more as needed
- Salt, to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, optional

> **Quantity note:** The potato amounts are scaled to approximately three servings. The original cook used a potato ricer and produced a successful purée, but its precise potato, butter, and cream measurements were not recorded here.

## Instructions

### Prepare the potatoes

1. Peel the potatoes and cut into evenly sized chunks. Place in a saucepan, cover with cold water by about an inch, and salt the water.
2. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Cook for **15–20 minutes**, or until a knife passes through the potatoes without resistance.
3. Drain thoroughly. Return the potatoes to the warm pot for **30–60 seconds** over low heat to evaporate excess moisture.
4. While still hot, pass the potatoes through a **potato ricer**. Keep warm while preparing the chicken, or finish the purée immediately before serving.

### Prepare and sear the chicken

5. If the breasts are very thick or uneven, butterfly them or gently pound to an even thickness. This helps prevent the outside from drying out while the center finishes cooking.
6. Pat dry and season both sides with salt and pepper. For a lightly thickened pan sauce, dredge very lightly in flour and shake off the excess. The flour dredge is an optional repeat-cook method; it was not conclusively documented for the original batch.
7. Heat oil and **1 tbsp butter** in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the chicken in batches if needed, allowing a golden crust to form without crowding the pan.
8. Lower the heat as necessary and cook until the thickest part reaches **165°F (74°C)**. Transfer to a plate. Avoid continuing to sear after the chicken is cooked through; this was the main issue in the original attempt.

### Build the Marsala sauce

9. Reduce the skillet to medium heat. Add **1 tbsp butter** and the sliced mushrooms. Cook until they release moisture and develop brown edges, approximately **5–8 minutes**.
10. Add the shallot, if using, and cook until softened, about **1–2 minutes**. Add garlic, if using, for the final **30 seconds**, just until fragrant.
11. Pour in the **Marsala wine** and scrape the browned bits from the pan. Simmer for **2–4 minutes** to reduce and concentrate its flavor.
12. Add the chicken broth and thyme, if using. Simmer uncovered until the sauce has reduced enough to **coat the back of a spoon**, roughly **5–10 minutes** depending on pan width and heat.
13. Taste before seasoning: stock and reduction can make the sauce salty. For a glossy finish, lower the heat and swirl in **1–2 tbsp cold butter** if needed. Return the chicken and any resting juices to the pan just long enough to warm through, without prolonged additional cooking.

### Finish the pommes purée

14. Warm the heavy cream separately. Fold the cubed butter into the hot riced potatoes until incorporated.
15. Add the warm cream gradually, stirring gently until the purée is smooth, rich, and soft enough to settle onto the plate. Add a little more cream if needed.
16. Season with salt. Avoid aggressive whisking or blending, which can make the potatoes gluey.
17. Plate the chicken with the pommes purée. Spoon the mushroom-Marsala sauce over the chicken, allowing some to mingle with the potatoes.

## Kitchen Notes

### What Worked

- **The Marsala sauce was the standout.** It had the right texture and a well-developed, flavorful finish.
- Previous pan sauces had sometimes been too thin; **this one reached the desired consistency**.
- The **pommes purée turned out beautifully**: smooth, buttery, and exactly the kind of accompaniment wanted for the sauce.
- The chicken developed an attractive browned exterior.

### What I'd Change

- **The chicken was slightly dry.** It spent too long searing, even though the outside looked good.
- On the next attempt, even out the thickness of the breasts and monitor internal temperature closely rather than relying on crust color or a fixed number of minutes.
- The original chicken's final temperature and exact searing duration were not recorded.

## Technique Notes

### Browning versus doneness

A golden crust develops through browning reactions at the chicken's surface, but **surface color is not a reliable indicator of internal doneness**. With large chicken breasts, using even thickness and a thermometer helps avoid overcooking while still developing a crust.

### Why the Marsala sauce thickens

As the wine and stock simmer, water evaporates and the remaining liquid becomes more concentrated. Mushroom juices, pan drippings, and any residual flour from the chicken can contribute body. Butter added near the end creates a richer, glossier finish. Aim for a sauce that lightly coats a spoon rather than reducing it into a sticky glaze.

### Why a potato ricer helps

A ricer breaks cooked potatoes into fine, even pieces without the intense shearing of a blender or food processor. Gentle mixing with butter and warm cream limits excessive starch release, helping the potatoes stay silky rather than gummy.
