## Overview

A roasted-garlic variation on French-style pommes purée: Yukon Gold potatoes passed through a ricer and gently enriched with butter and warm cream. An entire head of garlic is roasted until the cloves turn soft, sweet, and spreadable, then mashed into a paste and incorporated into the cream.

> **Recipe fidelity:** This roasted-garlic version was cooked on November 18, 2026. The measurements below are the working two-serving recipe, not documented amounts from that cook.

## Equipment

- Oven and small baking sheet
- Aluminum foil
- Knife and cutting board
- Medium saucepan for potatoes
- Potato ricer
- Small saucepan or microwave-safe container for warming cream
- Fork and small bowl for garlic paste
- Silicone spatula or wooden spoon

## Ingredients

For approximately two generous servings:

- 1 lb (450 g) Yukon Gold potatoes
- 1 whole head of garlic
- 1–2 tsp olive oil or avocado oil, for roasting
- 4–6 tbsp (57–85 g) unsalted butter, cubed
- 1/3–1/2 cup (80–120 ml) heavy cream, warmed; add gradually
- Salt, for the cooking water and final seasoning
- Freshly ground black pepper, optional

> **Starting point:** Use 4 tbsp butter and 1/3 cup cream, then adjust toward the richer end of the range. Begin with half to three-quarters of the roasted garlic and add the rest to taste. The exact amounts used in the November 18 cook were not recorded.

## Instructions

### Roast the garlic

1. Preheat the oven to **400°F (205°C)**.
2. Remove loose outer papery skin from the garlic head, leaving the bulb intact. Cut about 1/4 inch from the top to expose most of the cloves.
3. Place the head cut-side up on foil. Drizzle with 1–2 tsp oil and a small pinch of salt, then wrap securely.
4. Set on a baking sheet and roast for **40–50 minutes**, checking near the end. A large bulb may take a little longer. The cloves should be golden, very soft, and easily pierced with a knife.
5. Let cool until safe to handle. Squeeze the cloves from their skins and mash thoroughly with a fork into a smooth paste. Set aside.

### Cook and rice the potatoes

6. While the garlic roasts, peel the potatoes and cut into approximately 1 1/2-inch pieces.
7. Place in a saucepan, cover with cold water by about an inch, and salt the water. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer.
8. Cook for **15–20 minutes**, or until a knife passes through the pieces with almost no resistance.
9. Drain thoroughly. Return the potatoes to the warm, empty saucepan over low heat for **30–60 seconds**, stirring gently to evaporate surface moisture.
10. Pass the hot potatoes through a ricer. Avoid a blender or food processor, which can overwork the starch and make the purée gluey.

### Finish the purée

11. Warm 1/3 cup cream until hot but not boiling. Stir the roasted garlic paste into the cream, starting with half to three-quarters of the paste.
12. With the riced potatoes over very low heat, fold in 4 tbsp butter in small additions until incorporated.
13. Gradually fold in the warm garlic cream. Add more butter or warm cream as needed for a silky, soft consistency that gently settles on the plate.
14. Taste, season with salt, and add more roasted garlic paste if desired. Add black pepper only if it suits the dish. Serve warm.

## Technique Notes

### Why Roast the Garlic?

Raw garlic is sharp and pungent. Slow roasting softens the cloves and changes their flavor into something sweeter, mellower, and nuttier. The resulting paste blends readily into cream and potatoes without leaving harsh raw-garlic bites.

### Why Use a Ricer?

A ricer breaks cooked potatoes into fine, even pieces with relatively little mechanical mixing. Gentle folding afterward helps avoid a gummy texture caused by excessive starch release.

### Why Warm the Cream?

Warm cream incorporates more readily into hot potatoes and helps keep the finished purée smooth and warm. Add it gradually: the right amount depends on potato moisture and the texture you want.

## Storage and Reheating

Refrigerate leftovers promptly in a covered container. Reheat gently over low heat with a splash of milk or cream, stirring just until smooth and hot. Do not leave roasted garlic stored in oil at room temperature.
