## Overview

A rich, American-style garlic-Parmesan Alfredo designed to coat freshly extruded rigatoni. Butter and garlic provide the aromatic base, heavy cream creates body, and finely grated Parmesan adds saltiness and savory depth. Starchy pasta water is used at the end to loosen the sauce until it clings to the ridges and collects inside the tubes.

> **Recipe fidelity:** This dish was cooked on November 19, 2026. The quantities and times below are the working recipe from that day, not independently measured amounts from the cook. The pasta dough formula for this rigatoni batch was not recorded.

## Equipment

- KitchenAid stand mixer with Gourmet Pasta Press and rigatoni die, for the fresh pasta
- Large pot for boiling pasta
- Large skillet or sauté pan
- Whisk or silicone spatula

## Ingredients

For approximately two generous servings:

### Pasta

- About 8 oz (225 g) fresh homemade rigatoni
- Water for boiling
- Salt for the pasta water

> **Pasta dough note:** Rigatoni was made from scratch, but the exact flour, egg, water, yield, and extrusion settings for that batch were not documented. The 8 oz quantity is the working amount for the sauce, not a measured batch yield.

### Garlic-Parmesan Alfredo

- 3 tbsp (43 g) unsalted butter
- 2 garlic cloves, finely minced
- 3/4 cup (180 ml) heavy whipping cream
- 3/4 cup (approximately 65–75 g) freshly grated Parmesan
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- Salt, to taste after adding Parmesan
- Up to 1/2 cup reserved pasta water, added as needed

## Instructions

### Prepare the sauce

1. Bring a large pot of moderately salted water to a boil. Hold off on cooking the fresh rigatoni until the sauce is nearly ready.
2. Set a large skillet over **medium-low heat**. Melt the butter, then add the minced garlic. Stir for **45–60 seconds**, until fragrant but not browned.
3. Pour in the heavy cream and stir to combine. Bring to a **gentle simmer** and cook for **2–3 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until slightly thickened. Avoid an aggressive boil.
4. Turn the heat to low or take the skillet off the burner. Add the finely grated Parmesan in **3–4 additions**, stirring until incorporated after each. Add black pepper. Taste before adding salt because the cheese is already salty.

### Cook the fresh rigatoni

5. Add the fresh rigatoni to the boiling water and stir gently to prevent sticking.
6. Begin tasting after **3–4 minutes**. Continue cooking as necessary until the pasta is cooked through but pleasantly firm. Extruded rigatoni can have thicker walls than fresh rolled pasta, so cooking time varies.
7. Reserve at least **1/2 cup pasta water** before draining.

### Finish and serve

8. Add the drained rigatoni to the skillet with the Alfredo sauce. Toss gently over low heat for **30–60 seconds**.
9. Add reserved pasta water **1 tablespoon at a time**, tossing until the sauce is glossy and coats the pasta without becoming pasty. The sauce will thicken slightly as it cools.
10. Taste for seasoning and serve immediately, optionally with extra Parmesan and black pepper.

## Technique Notes

### Why Add Parmesan Off the Heat?

Excessive heat can make melted hard cheese clump or turn the sauce grainy. Lower heat and gradual additions make the sauce easier to incorporate smoothly. Finely grating Parmesan also helps it disperse.

### Why Reserve Pasta Water?

Starchy pasta water loosens the cream sauce and helps it distribute around the ridges and inside the rigatoni. Add it gradually: the target is a silky coating on the pasta, not an unusually thick sauce sitting in the skillet.

### Why Finish Pasta in the Sauce?

A brief toss lets the sauce coat the pasta evenly. Fresh pasta can absorb some moisture, so keep the sauce slightly looser than the final texture you want.

### Extruded vs. Rolled Pasta

Rigatoni is **extruded**: an auger pushes dough through a shaped die to create hollow tubes. The pasta's wall thickness and dough hydration influence its cooking time. Unlike rolled pasta, extrusion generally calls for a firmer dough that can hold its shape after leaving the die.

## Troubleshooting

- **Sauce too thick:** Add warm reserved pasta water a tablespoon at a time.
- **Sauce too thin:** Before adding cheese, gently simmer the cream a little longer; once assembled, allow brief gentle heating while tossing.
- **Sauce grainy:** Remove from heat and whisk in a little warm cream or pasta water. This may improve the texture but may not fully reverse separation.
- **Sauce too salty:** Add a little unsalted cream or more unsalted pasta; avoid reducing further.
- **Sauce bland:** Taste for salt first, then consider a little more Parmesan or black pepper.
