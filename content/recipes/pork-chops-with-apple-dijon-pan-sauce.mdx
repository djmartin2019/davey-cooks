## Overview

Bone-in center-loin pork chops seared in cast iron with caramelized apple wedges and an apple cider, shallot, thyme, Dijon, and butter pan sauce.

The ingredients and instructions below are the version as actually cooked. Proposed changes are listed separately under **Next Time** and have not been tested.

## Ingredients

As cooked:

- 2 thick bone-in center-loin pork chops (purchased H-E-B package: 1.57 lb total)
- Salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp avocado oil, or as needed
- 2 apples (Honeycrisp or Pink Lady), cored and cut into 8 wedges each
- 1 shallot, finely diced
- 3–4 fresh thyme sprigs
- 1/2 cup nonalcoholic apple cider (Louisburg Old Fashioned Apple Cider was considered and photographed)
- 1/2 cup low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 2 tbsp cold unsalted butter
- Optional: 1/4 tsp apple cider vinegar, only if sauce needs acidity
- Mashed potatoes, optional side (planned but forgotten on serving night)

## Instructions

As cooked:

1. Pat chops dry and season with salt and pepper. Ideally salt 30–60 minutes ahead and refrigerate until cooking.
2. Heat cast iron over medium-high with avocado oil. Sear approximately 3–4 minutes on the first side and 3 minutes on the second, then adjust heat and continue until the thickest portion reaches **145°F**; avoid touching bone with thermometer. Rest at least 3 minutes.
3. Leave approximately 1 tbsp of pan fat. Brown apple wedges cut-side down over medium heat, about 2–3 minutes per side. Remove while still holding shape.
4. Cook shallot 1–2 minutes in same skillet. Deglaze with cider and broth, scraping up fond. Add thyme and simmer 4–6 minutes, reducing by about a third.
5. Whisk in Dijon and taste. Add vinegar in tiny increments only if needed.
6. Reduce heat; whisk in cold butter, one piece at a time.
7. Return apples just long enough to coat and warm (about 30–60 seconds), unless they need extra time to soften. Serve with chops and sauce.

## Kitchen Notes

### What Worked

- Chops and apples developed attractive golden-brown crusts; apple skins softened nicely.
- Sauce tasted like **sweet French onion soup**, which was enjoyable and **not too sweet**.
- As a complete dish, the sauce tied everything together.

### What I'd Change

- The sauce lacked a distinct apple identity.
- The pork was a little tough and comparatively mild in flavor; exact final internal temperature was **not recorded**.
- Apple wedges were soft overall, but their centers remained somewhat firm. Core trimming and/or longer gentle cooking may help.
- Sauce could have used more salt and pepper.
- Finished dish looked better than it tasted; mashed potatoes were forgotten, so plating was minimal.

## Next Time

> **Not yet tested.** These are proposed changes for the next attempt. They do not replace the as-cooked quantities above.

Aim for stronger apple identity and more tender pork:

- Try well-marbled pork rib chops instead of lean center-loin chops.
- Use **3/4 cup apple cider and 1/4 cup chicken broth**.
- Reduce Dijon to **1 tsp** so it doesn't dominate.
- Use **1/2 shallot**, **2 thyme sprigs**, **2 tbsp cold butter**.
- Add **1/4–1/2 finely diced apple** to the sauce with the shallot; reserve larger wedges for plating.
- Start with **1/2 tsp apple cider vinegar only if needed**, then adjust by taste.
- Consider adding 1–2 tbsp fresh cider near the end to restore brighter apple flavor.
- Core apples thoroughly. Use 8 wedges for plating and cook them gently until tender; 16ths are an alternative if quicker cooking is preferred.
- Season the finished sauce deliberately with salt and pepper.
- Record chop temperature and resting time to diagnose toughness.
